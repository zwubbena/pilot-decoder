The aiming point starts just past the runway numbers, at 250 feet (0.0762 kilometers). That figure estimates where the numbers end on a runway with threshold stripes: the stripes start 20 feet in and run 150 feet, then come a 20-foot gap and 60-foot numbers. Instrument runways also carry a painted aiming point marking about 1,000 feet past the threshold (Aeronautical Information Manual 2-3-3). Because of the flare, the wheels touch down beyond the aiming point (Airplane Flying Handbook).

When checked, the airplane holds a constant 3° glide path to the aiming point and the height follows the distance. The Airplane Flying Handbook (FAA-H-8083-3C) defines a stabilized approach as a constant-angle glide path to a point on the runway, and Aeronautical Information Manual 2-1-2 shows 3° as the on-glide-path angle for runway glide path lights.

Height is above the runway. For an altimeter reading, subtract the runway's elevation first.
