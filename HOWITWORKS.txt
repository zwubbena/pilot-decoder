CLOUD DECODER — HOW IT WORKS, IN PLAIN ENGLISH
A walk-through from photo upload to results
================================================================

For: pilotdecoder.com/cloud-decoder
Keep this handy for explaining the tool to anyone who asks.


----------------------------------------------------------------
THE 20-SECOND VERSION
----------------------------------------------------------------
You upload a photo of the sky, and it tells you what kind of
clouds are in it, from a pilot's point of view. Behind the scenes
it hands your photo to Claude — an AI given a detailed cloud-
identification checklist. Claude names the clouds, rates how sure
it is, and explains what it saw. It is built so it cannot run up a
big bill and so my private key stays protected.


----------------------------------------------------------------
THE STEP-BY-STEP (UPLOAD -> RESULTS)
----------------------------------------------------------------

1. YOU PICK A PHOTO
   On the page, you choose a sky photo. Your browser reads the
   photo right there on your device and turns it into a block of
   text-like data it can send over the internet.

2. FIRST SAFETY NET — SIZE
   If the photo is huge (over about 5 MB), it is turned away
   immediately with a "use a smaller photo" message — before
   anything costs a cent.

3. THE PHOTO GOES TO THE "WORKER"
   This is a little program running on Cloudflare's servers — not
   the web page, not my Mac. Think of it as a receptionist sitting
   between the public website and the AI. Its job is to take the
   request, add my secret key privately, and pass it along — so my
   paid key never lives on the public web page where someone could
   grab it.

4. SECOND SAFETY NET — RATE LIMIT
   The receptionist checks how many times that visitor has asked in
   the last minute. If someone is hammering it (more than 15 times
   a minute), they are politely told to wait. That stops one person
   from spamming it and burning through my credits.

5. THE WORKER HANDS THE PHOTO TO CLAUDE
   It attaches my private key (kept encrypted on Cloudflare, never
   shown) and sends the photo to Claude Sonnet 5, along with a
   detailed instruction sheet — essentially a pilot's cloud-
   identification manual.

6. CLAUDE WORKS THROUGH IT LIKE A CHECKLIST
   This is the heart of it. Claude does not just glance and guess.
   It follows a strict order:
     - Reads the photo first (Is the horizon visible? Where is the
       sun? Is it hazy?).
     - Writes down what it sees BEFORE naming anything — altitude,
       texture, shading. This ordering matters: it commits to its
       observations first, so the answer comes FROM the evidence
       rather than being justified after the fact.
     - Tests for dangerous storm clouds first, then works down
       through the other types.
     - Applies tie-breaker tests for clouds that look alike (the
       classic mix-ups).
     - Names up to three cloud types, and for each one rates its
       confidence — high, medium, or low — using fixed rules (for
       example, "high" only if the tell-tale feature is clearly
       visible in the photo).

7. THE ANSWER COMES BACK AND GETS DOUBLE-CHECKED
   Claude returns everything as tidy structured data. The worker
   sanity-checks it — throws out any name that is not one of the 11
   official cloud types, removes duplicates — and passes the clean
   result to the page.

8. THE PAGE SHOWS THE RESULT
   You see:
     - the cloud type(s),
     - a colored confidence pill (green = high, amber = medium,
       red = low),
     - and an expandable "How this was identified" that reveals
       Claude's reasoning: altitude, texture, shading, and a plain
       sentence explaining what it saw (and anything it could NOT
       tell from the photo).

9. THE COST CEILING
   It is ONE Claude call per photo — a few cents at most — and
   there is a hard $5-a-month cap on the whole thing. No matter how
   many people use it, it physically cannot spend more than that.


----------------------------------------------------------------
THE ONE THING THAT CHANGED RECENTLY
----------------------------------------------------------------
It used to run each photo past TWO AI models and compare their
answers. Now it is ONE model doing one careful pass. Why? The two
models were basically grading with different rulers, which made
their "agreement" noisier than it looked. One model following one
rigorous checklist is more consistent — and it costs half as much.
