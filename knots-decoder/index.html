Knots Decoder

Knots to miles per hour, nautical miles to statute miles — reversible converters that show the factor, the arithmetic, and why the numbers land where they do. Built on the exact definitions: 1 NM = 1,852 m, 1 mi = 1,609.344 m.

01

The words, before the math

Your METAR reports visibility in statute miles while your airspeed indicator reads knots. These two converters bridge the systems — and show the arithmetic instead of hiding it.

  • Knot (kt) — a speed: 1 nautical mile traveled in 1 hour. Never a distance.
  • Nautical mile (NM) — exactly 1,852 meters, close to one minute of latitude, which is why charts and navigation use it.
  • Statute mile (mi) — exactly 1,609.344 meters (5,280 ft), the everyday land mile of road signs and METAR visibility.
  • Conversion factor — 1,852 ÷ 1,609.344 = 1.150779448. One number converts both speed and distance, because a knot is just a nautical mile with a clock attached.

04

Why these units differ

The nautical mile is built from the planet: one minute of latitude is very nearly 1,852 meters, so distances in nautical miles convert cleanly to angles on a chart — which is why aviation and marine navigation, groundspeeds, and winds aloft all use nautical miles and knots. The statute mile is built from land surveying: 5,280 feet, standardized in 1959 as exactly 1,609.344 meters, and it survives in aviation anywhere the ground rules — most notably METAR visibility.

Because both conversions divide the same two meter definitions, one factor serves both: multiply knots by 1.150779448 to get miles per hour, and multiply nautical miles by the same number to get statute miles. Going the other way divides by it (or multiplies by 0.868976242, the same thing).
